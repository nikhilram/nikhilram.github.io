# Scientific Leadership

## Connecting science, data, clinical insight, and strategy

My work sits at the intersection of **scientific discovery, translational research, data-driven analysis, clinical medicine, and scientific communication**. Across multidisciplinary programs, I have worked with academic researchers, clinicians, computational scientists, experimental teams, and industry collaborators to move complex scientific questions from hypothesis through evidence, interpretation, publication, and translation.

I am particularly effective in environments where the science is complex, the data are heterogeneous, and progress depends on connecting people and disciplines that do not naturally speak the same language.

---

## Scientific Strategy & Program Leadership

I translate broad scientific questions into tractable research strategies and integrate experimental, computational, clinical, and translational perspectives to move programs forward.

* Develop research strategies spanning biological discovery, computational analysis, and translational validation
* Frame scientific questions, hypotheses, and experimental approaches across multidisciplinary programs
* Identify the analyses and evidence required to distinguish compelling findings from interesting observations
* Connect individual projects to broader scientific and translational objectives
* Evaluate emerging technologies and methodologies for their potential to address unmet research or clinical needs
* Align investigators and collaborators around scientific priorities, interpretation, and next steps

---

## Publications & Scientific Narrative Strategy

I bring a data-first approach to scientific communication: understanding the evidence deeply enough to determine what the data actually support, then building a coherent narrative around the strongest scientific story.

* Lead manuscript development from data interpretation through final submission
* Develop scientific narratives from complex, multidisciplinary datasets
* Coordinate contributions across computational, experimental, clinical, and industry collaborators
* Translate technical findings into clear messages for scientific, clinical, and broader audiences
* Identify the central scientific advance within large or fragmented bodies of work
* Develop publication and funding narratives that connect scientific novelty with significance and impact
* Present complex findings to audiences ranging from specialist researchers to interdisciplinary and executive stakeholders

My publication record spans infectious disease, immunology, genomics, diagnostics, antimicrobial susceptibility testing, and computational biology, including work published in *Science*, *Nature Microbiology*, *The Lancet Infectious Diseases*, *Nucleic Acids Research*, and *Clinical Infectious Diseases*.

## Translational Research & Clinical Science

My research experience spans discovery science and clinically oriented research, with a particular focus on **infectious disease, host response, diagnostics, antimicrobial resistance, and immune-mediated disease**.

### Host Response & Infectious Disease

I have worked with clinical cohorts, biobanks, molecular datasets, and experimental systems to investigate host-pathogen interactions and identify biologically and clinically meaningful signals.

Examples include:

* Host-response signatures for infectious disease and COVID-19
* Biomarker development and clinical phenotype association
* Molecular characterization of sepsis and bloodstream infections
* Immunological heterogeneity and bactericidal activity
* Extracellular DNA biology and neutrophil extracellular traps

### Diagnostics & Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing

I have contributed to the development and evaluation of approaches intended to make infectious-disease diagnostics and antimicrobial susceptibility testing faster, more quantitative, and more clinically actionable.

My work includes molecular and phenotypic approaches to antimicrobial susceptibility testing, quantitative single-cell analysis, image-based phenotyping, and rapid testing strategies for organisms including *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.

A particular translational challenge I have worked on is **rapid AST for *N. gonorrhoeae***—an area where AST is not routinely available in clinical practice and where genuinely new technologies are needed to make susceptibility-guided treatment practical and potentially reduce unnecessary reliance on ceftriaxone.

## Cross-Functional Scientific Leadership

My work has consistently required me to operate across organizational and disciplinary boundaries—connecting **academic research, industry partners, clinical specialists, computational scientists, and experimental researchers** around complex scientific questions.

### Academia ↔ Industry

I have collaborated with biotechnology and diagnostic companies to evaluate scientific questions, technologies, and translational opportunities at the interface of research and product development.

These collaborations have included work with organizations such as **Combinate** and **Inflammatix**, providing experience working across different scientific and organizational environments where research must ultimately connect to technology, clinical utility, and real-world implementation.

### Research ↔ Clinical Medicine

My translational work has involved collaboration with clinicians and physician-scientists across multiple specialties, including **infectious disease, immunology, rheumatology, and cardiology**, among others.

These collaborations have helped bridge biological discovery with clinical context—from defining clinically relevant questions and interpreting patient-derived data to evaluating whether molecular findings have meaningful implications for diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment.

### Computational ↔ Experimental Science

I routinely work between computational and experimental disciplines, translating biological questions into quantitative analyses and translating computational findings into experimentally testable hypotheses.

### Discovery ↔ Translation

My experience spans basic biological investigation, technology development, clinical research, and scientific communication. This allows me to evaluate a research program from multiple perspectives:

**What is biologically interesting?**
**What is technically feasible?**
**What does the evidence actually support?**
**What matters clinically?**
**What could ultimately be useful outside the laboratory?**

### Science ↔ Strategy

I am often most valuable at the point where scientific complexity creates a strategic problem: determining which findings matter, what evidence is missing, which experiments or analyses should come next, and how the resulting story should be communicated to different stakeholders.

## How I Work

I tend to be most effective when a problem does not fit neatly into a single discipline or job description.

I bring together:

**Scientific depth**
The ability to interrogate the underlying biology rather than relying solely on high-level summaries.

**Analytical thinking**
The ability to determine what the data actually show, identify patterns and inconsistencies, and build quantitative evidence.

**Strategic judgment**
The ability to distinguish what is interesting from what is consequential—and determine what evidence is needed next.

**Communication**
The ability to turn complex science into a narrative that different stakeholders can understand and act upon.

**Execution**
The ability to move from an open-ended scientific problem to a concrete analytical, experimental, publication, or funding strategy.
